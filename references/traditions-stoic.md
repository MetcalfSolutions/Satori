## 1. Classical Stoicism (Primary)

### Core Concepts
- **The Dichotomy of Control** — Some things are up to us (judgments, desires, aversions, actions); most things are not (external outcomes, other people's opinions, health, fortune). Suffering arises from mistaking what is not up to us for what is.
- **Virtue as the Sole Good** — True flourishing (*eudaimonia*) depends solely on character: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance. External circumstances are "preferred or dispreferred indifferents."
- **Amor Fati** — Love of fate. Not passive endurance, but choosing to embrace whatever occurs as material for virtue.
- **Premeditatio Malorum (Negative Visualization)** — Anticipating hardship not to induce dread, but to disarm catastrophe and appreciate what is present.
- **The View from Above** — Zooming out to cosmic scale to dissolve trivial vanity and restore proportion.
- **Prosochē** — Continuous Stoic mindfulness; vigilance over one's own judgments in real time.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Epictetus** — *Discourses* and *Enchiridion* (freed slave; uncompromising emphasis on moral will).
- **Marcus Aurelius** — *Meditations* (Roman emperor; private journal on duty, transience, and integrity).
- **Seneca** — *Letters from a Stoic*, *On the Shortness of Life* (statesman; psychological realism).
- **Pierre Hadot** — *Philosophy as a Way of Life*, *The Inner Citadel*.

### When to Draw On This
- Catastrophizing, performance anxiety, obsessing over future uncertainties.
- Paralyzed by resentment toward unfair institutional or external circumstances.
- Restoring sovereign agency when external conditions cannot be altered.

### Sample Language
> *"The Stoics had a razor for this: what part of this is actually within your hands right now, and what part are you asking of reality that it hasn't agreed to give you?"*  
> *"What if the obstacle isn't blocking the work — what if how you handle this is the work?"*

## 2. Existentialism & Frankl's Logotherapy

### Core Concepts
- **Existence Precedes Essence (Sartre)** — Humans are not born with a pre-programmed purpose; we define who we are through lived choices and commitments.
- **The Absurd (Camus)** — The clash between human longing for inherent meaning and the silent, indifferent universe. The response is not despair or suicide, but defiant vitality and creative freedom (*"One must imagine Sisyphus happy"*).
- **Logotherapy & Will to Meaning (Frankl)** — Meaning is found not through self-indulgence, but through responsibility:
  1. What we create or do.
  2. What we experience or encounter (love, beauty, nature).
  3. The attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering (*"When we can no longer change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves"*).
- **Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi)** — Deceiving oneself into believing one had no choice, playing a fixed role to escape freedom.
- **Anxiety as the Dizziness of Freedom (Kierkegaard)** — Dread is the recognition of our own limitless possibility.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Viktor Frankl** — *Man's Search for Meaning*.
- **Albert Camus** — *The Myth of Sisyphus*, *The Plague*.
- **Jean-Paul Sartre** — *Existentialism Is a Humanism*, *Being and Nothingness*.
- **Rollo May & Irvin Yalom** — *Existential Psychotherapy* (death, freedom, isolation, meaninglessness).

### When to Draw On This
- Sudden mid-career crises, loss of purpose, or existential vacuum.
- When unavoidable tragedy occurs and false optimism feels insulting.
- Unmasking self-victimization and reclaiming personal responsibility.

## 3. Foundations of Modern Psychology

### Core Frameworks
- **Cognitive-Behavioral Architecture (Beck & Ellis)** — Thoughts, feelings, and behaviors form a dynamic loop. We are troubled not by events, but by the interpretations we assign to them (direct descendant of Epictetus).
- **Acceptance & Commitment Therapy (ACT - Hayes)**:
  - *Cognitive Defusion*: Stepping back to observe thoughts as mental events (*"I am noticing the thought that I am failing"*).
  - *Values vs. Goals*: Values are directions (like heading West); goals are checkpoints.
  - *Psychological Flexibility*: The ability to feel discomfort while taking values-based action.
- **Attachment Theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth, Wallin)** — Early relational templates (secure, anxious, avoidant, disorganized) shape adult intimacy, conflict, and self-soothing.
- **Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)** — Sustained human flourishing requires three core psychological needs: Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness.

## 4. Phenomenology & Embodied Sense

### Core Concepts
- **The Lived Body (Merleau-Ponty)** — We do not merely *have* a body; we *are* our bodies. Perception is somatic before it is intellectual.
- **Focusing & The Felt Sense (Eugene Gendlin)** — A physical, bodily sense of an entire situation or problem. The felt shift occurs when the body recognizes the truth before the cognitive mind articulates it.
- **Being-in-the-World (Heidegger)** — We are always already thrown into a context of concern, relationships, and mortality.

## 5. Evolutionary Psychology & Behavioral Science

### Core Frameworks
- **The Elephant and the Rider (Jonathan Haidt)** — The emotional, intuitive brain is an elephant; the conscious, reasoning mind is a rider. Sustainable movement comes from aligning with the elephant, not shouting at it.
- **Dual Process Theory (Kahneman System 1 & System 2)** — Fast, associative, heuristic thinking versus slow, effortful, deliberative inquiry.
- **Loss Aversion & Sunk Cost Fallacy** — Human brains feel losses twice as intensely as equivalent gains, leading to irrational preservation of failed projects or unfulfilling relationships.
