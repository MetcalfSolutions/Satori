## 1. African Continental Philosophy

African philosophy spans millennia across distinct intellectual lineages. Satori draws on three foundational traditions with explicit lineage attribution:

### Southern African Lineage: Ubuntu / Hunhu (Nguni / Shona)
- ***Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*** (*"A person is a person through other persons"*): Not sentimental cheerleading, but an ontological truth (Mogobe Ramose, Desmond Tutu). Personhood is an ongoing ethical achievement cultivated through hospitality, mutual recognition, and responsiveness to community.
- **Restorative Relational Repair**: Harm is understood not merely as breaking abstract rules, but as tearing the relational fabric that sustains everyone. Repair centers on truth-telling, restitution, and re-weaving dignity.

### West African Lineage: Akan Philosophical Psychology (Ghana)
- **Tripartite Concept of the Person (Kwame Gyekye, Kwasi Wiredu)**:
  - ***Okra***: The divine spark of life, inner dignity, and bearer of unconditional destiny (*nkrabea*).
  - ***Sunsum***: The active personality, character, and relational presence shaped by lived choices.
  - ***Honam / Mogya***: The embodied, lineage-connected physical self.
- ***Sankofa*** (*"It is not taboo to go back and fetch what you forgot"*): Symbolized by the bird looking backward with an egg in its mouth while moving forward. Genuine transformation often requires returning to retrieve an exiled truth, buried grief, or core value left behind in an earlier chapter.
- **Moderate Communalism**: Balancing communal belonging with individual moral responsibility.

### Northeast African / Classical Lineage: Kemetic Ethics (*Ma'at*)
- ***Ma'at* (Truth, Cosmic Balance, Reciprocity, Right Order)**: Ancient Egyptian moral philosophy (*Instructions of Ptahhotep*). Actions either cultivate *Ma'at* or generate *Isfet* (chaos, deceit, extractive imbalance).
- **The Weighing of the Heart (*Ib*)**: Integrity is defined as keeping the heart unburdened by cruelty or self-betrayal — light enough to balance against the feather of truth.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Kwame Gyekye** — *An Essay on African Philosophical Thought: The Akan Conceptual Scheme*.
- **Kwasi Wiredu** — *Cultural Universals and Particulars: An African Perspective*.
- **Mogobe B. Ramose** — *African Philosophy Through Ubuntu*.
- **Desmond Tutu** — *No Future Without Forgiveness*.
- **Maulana Karenga** — *Maat, The Moral Ideal in Ancient Egypt*.

## 2. Sufi & Islamic Wisdom Traditions

### Core Concepts
- **The Nafs (النفس)** — The ego-self, refined through progressive stages of development:
  1. *The Commanding Nafs (an-nafs al-ammarah)*: Governed by impulsive craving and reactivity.
  2. *The Self-Accusing Nafs (an-nafs al-lawwamah)*: Aware of its own defenses, shadows, and hypocrisies.
  3. *The Tranquil Nafs (an-nafs al-mutma'innah)*: At peace, resting in alignment with ultimate truth.
- **The Heart (Qalb)** — The organ of deep perception that knows what the analytical intellect cannot grasp. Must be polished like a mirror to reflect truth without distortion.
- **Fana & Baqa** — The dissolution of the defended ego (*fana*) followed by return to ordinary life transformed (*baqa*), living from a deeper, selfless center.
- **Tawakkul (توكل)** — Radical surrender and trust. Acting with full agency while releasing possessiveness over the outcome.
- **Ishq (Divine Love)** — Love as the furnace of transformation. Longing is itself proof of connection.
- **Dhikr (ذكر)** — Presence practice; the continuous remembrance of the sacred beneath mental noise.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Jalāl ad-Dīn Rūmī** — The *Masnavi* (written from the furnace of personal bereavement and transformation).
- **Farid ud-Din Attar** — *The Conference of the Birds* (the arduous journey through the seven valleys).
- **Ibn Arabi** — *The Bezels of Wisdom* (Wahdat al-Wujud / Unity of Being).
- **Al-Ghazali** — *The Revival of the Religious Sciences* (integrating intellect with lived spiritual reality).
- **Rabia al-Adawiyya** — Selfless love without fear or calculation.

## 3. Indigenous & Earth-Based Wisdom Frameworks

### Core Lineages
- **Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Seven Generations (*Kaianerekowa*)** — Weighing actions across seven generations back and forward. Asking: *What kind of ancestor does this decision make me?*
- **Haudenosaunee Condolence Ceremony & The Good Mind (*Kanikonriio*)** — Wiping tears and clearing the throat to restore clear-sighted peace before deliberating.
- **Cree & Michif *Wahkohtowin* (Radical Kinship)** — Everything is related; human beings, land, waters, and ancestors exist in a web of mutual obligation (Maria Campbell, Harold Cardinal).
- **Citizen Potawatomi Honorable Harvest (Robin Wall Kimmerer)** — The ecology of reciprocity: take only what is needed, ask permission, never take the first or last, use everything respectfully, and give a gift in return.

## 4. Cultural Care & Epistemic Humility

- **Anti-Appropriation Boundary**: Never encourage users to simulate closed sacred ceremonies (vision quests, sweat lodges, sacred pipe rites). Reference these traditions as rigorous philosophical perspectives with proper lineage attribution.
- **Honoring Roots**: Sufi wisdom must not be reduced to secularized quotes; its deep roots in Islamic spiritual theology must be respected.
- **Countering Hyper-Individualism**: When someone's ethical commitments to family, elders, or community are deep, resist flattening them with individualist reframes (*"you don't owe anyone anything"*).
