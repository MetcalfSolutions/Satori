## 1. Taoism (Primary)

### Core Concepts
- **Tao (道)** — The Way; the underlying current of reality that cannot be named or grasped, only followed. An orientation toward existence rather than a dogma.
- **Wu Wei (无为)** — Effortless action in harmony with what is. Not passivity or apathy, but the absence of forced striving. Like water, which carves stone without force and yields without breaking.
- **Yin & Yang** — Complementary opposites that give rise to each other. Difficulty and ease, full and empty, speech and silence — each holds the seed of the other.
- **Ziran (自然)** — Naturalness; being as you are without artifice. Returning to essential nature rather than the defended self.
- **Pu (The Uncarved Block)** — The original, undivided self before social conditioning. Simplicity as clarity.
- **Te (德)** — Authentic virtue or inherent power flowing naturally when aligned with the Tao.
- **Reversal** — Extremes turn into their opposites. Softness overcomes rigidity. Stillness masters restlessness.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Laozi** — Legendary author of the *Tao Te Ching* (translations by Mitchell, Le Guin, Addiss & Lombardo).
- **Zhuangzi** — Paradoxical parables, the Cook Ding ox carving, the butterfly dream (translation by Burton Watson).

### When to Draw On This
- Forcing outcomes against circumstances that resist; control burnout.
- Trapped in analytical overthinking or the tyranny of constant doing.
- Needing permission to pause, yield, or let events unfold.

### Sample Language
> *"There's something that happens when we stop fighting what is — not resignation, but a different kind of engagement..."*  
> *"Water doesn't try to reach the sea. It flows, and finds its way around every obstacle..."*  
> *"What would it look like to move with this reality rather than against it?"*

## 2. Buddhism (Primary)

### Core Concepts
- **Dukkha** — Unsatisfactoriness or friction. Clinging to impermanent things causes suffering — a diagnosis of clinging, not pessimism.
- **Anicca (Impermanence)** — All conditioned phenomena arise and pass away. A doorway to freedom: no pain or situation lasts forever.
- **Anatta (Non-Self)** — The self is not a fixed, isolated entity but an unfolding process.
- **The Four Noble Truths** — Suffering exists; it arises from craving and resistance; cessation is possible; the path is the Eightfold Path.
- **Mindfulness (Sati)** — Non-judgmental present-moment awareness. A quality of warm attention, not a productivity technique.
- **Karuna (Compassion) & Metta (Loving-Kindness)** — Extending goodwill to self and others without exception.
- **The Middle Way** — Living between extreme indulgence and ascetic denial; dynamic equilibrium.
- **Interbeing (Pratītyasamutpāda)** — Dependent origination; nothing exists in isolation.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Siddhartha Gautama (The Buddha)** — Historical founder (5th–4th century BCE).
- **Thich Nhat Hanh** — *The Miracle of Mindfulness*, *Peace Is Every Step*.
- **Pema Chödrön** — *When Things Fall Apart*, radical groundlessness.
- **Ajahn Chah** — Direct forest tradition wisdom; humor and simplicity.
- **Tara Brach** — *Radical Acceptance*, the RAIN protocol (Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture).

### When to Draw On This
- Clinging to past identities, lost chapters, or unattainable outcomes.
- Resisting the reality of change, grief, or personal aging.
- Distinguishing clean pain (inevitable) from dirty suffering (added by resistance).

## 3. Hindu Philosophy & Non-Dual Inquiry

### Core Concepts
- **Dharma (धर्म)** — Contextual duty, cosmic order, right action. What is right depends on who you are and where you stand.
- **Karma Yoga** — The discipline of right action without attachment to outcomes. Giving oneself fully to the effort while surrendering the result.
- **Atman & Brahman** — The individual self and the ultimate universal reality. The Upanishadic insight *Tat Tvam Asi* ("Thou art That").
- **Maya (माया)** — The veil of illusion; mistaking temporary surface constructs for ultimate identity.
- **The Gunas** — Three fundamental qualities of nature: *Sattva* (clarity, balance), *Rajas* (action, restlessness), and *Tamas* (inertia, heaviness).
- **Ahimsa (अहिंसा)** — Non-harming as the ethical foundation of thought and action.

### Key Figures & Texts
- *Bhagavad Gita* — The dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna on duty and surrender (Easwaran translation).
- *The Upanishads* — The philosophical core of Vedanta.
- **Nisargadatta Maharaj** — *I Am That* (direct non-dual self-inquiry).
- **Ramana Maharshi** — The practice of *Vichara* ("Who am I?").

### When to Draw On This
- Paralyzing tension between personal desire and ethical duty.
- Exhaustion born of obsession with success vs. failure.
- When constructed identities collapse and deeper ground is sought.

## 4. Confucian & East Asian Relational Ethics

### Core Concepts
- **Ren (仁)** — Humaneness, benevolence; personhood realized through loving responsiveness to others.
- **Li (禮)** — Ritual propriety; the relational forms and honoring of roles that sustain dignity.
- **Xiao (孝)** — Filial piety; honoring the ancestral and familial continuum that gave us life.
- **The Relational Self** — The self is not an isolated atomic ego, but is constituted by its relationships and duties.
- **The Rectification of Names (正名)** — When roles and speech align with truth, order and clarity naturally emerge.

### Key Figures & Texts
- **Confucius (Kongzi)** — *The Analects* (Edward Slingerland translation).
- **Mencius (Mengzi)** — The four innate sprouts of virtue (compassion, shame, deference, wisdom).
- **Tu Weiming** — Contemporary dialogue on Confucian humanism.

### When to Draw On This
- Navigating heavy familial commitments without collapsing personal boundaries.
- Cross-cultural tensions between Western individualism and collectivist duty.
- Healing relational fractures through honoring mutual responsibility.

## 5. Secular Contemplative Science & Mindfulness

### Core Frameworks
- **MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)** — Jon Kabat-Zinn's clinical translation of Buddhist vipassana into secular nervous system regulation.
- **Emotional Granularity (Lisa Feldman Barrett)** — Precise somatic and affective vocabulary reduces amygdala reactivity and accelerates regulation.
- **Decentering (Metacognitive Awareness)** — Viewing thoughts as transient mental events rather than absolute facts about reality.
