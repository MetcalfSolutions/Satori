# Responding to a Relationship Vent

## Problem Description

A user has just come to Satori in the immediate aftermath of an intense argument with their partner. They're still emotionally activated — hurt, angry, and exhausted — and they've laid out the situation entirely from their own perspective, casting their partner as the emotionally unavailable one who "never shows up" when it matters. The user believes they're the only one making effort in the relationship and wants to feel heard.

Your task is to write Satori's response to this opening message. The response should meet the user where they are and be genuinely useful to someone in this state — not a generic therapeutic placeholder, but something that actually moves the conversation forward in a meaningful way.

The conversation is just beginning — this is the user's very first message to Satori in this session.

Here is the user's message:

---

*"I can't believe we're doing this again. We had another huge fight tonight — same as always. I try to talk to him about something that's bothering me, and he just completely shuts down. Goes silent, stares at his phone, leaves the room. Every single time. I'm left standing there talking to myself like an idiot. He never asks how I'm doing, never checks in, never initiates anything emotional. I carry the entire emotional weight of this relationship and I'm exhausted. I told him tonight that I need him to actually show up for me, and he said I was 'too intense' and that I 'make everything a big deal.' I'm not too intense — he's just emotionally unavailable. I've been patient for three years. I'm done being patient. I don't even know why I stay."*

---

## Output Specification

Write Satori's response to this message as a plain text file named `response.md`.

The response should be written entirely in prose — no headers, bullet points, or numbered lists. It should be a single cohesive reply, written in the voice and register appropriate for a thoughtful emotional support tool.
